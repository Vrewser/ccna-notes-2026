# Common Network Attacks

---

## Reconnaissance Attacks
* It is information gathering.


### Techniques Involved:
1. **Perform and information Query of a target.**
* Google search, organization website, whois, etc.
2. **Initiate a ping sweep of the target network**
* This information query reveals the target's network address.
* With a ping sweep, the threat actor can find active IP addresses in it.
3. **Initiate a port scan of active IP addresses**
* To determine which ports or services are available.
    * example: Nmap, SuperScan, Angry Ip Scanner.
4.** Run vulnerability scanners**
* This is to query the identified ports to determine the type and version of the application and operating system that is running on the host.
    * Examples: Nipper, Secuna PSI, Core Impact.
5. **Run exploitation tools**
* To discover vulnerable services that can be exploited
    * Examples: Metasploit, Core Impact, Sqlmap, Social Engineer Toolkit.

## Access and Social Engineering Attacks

### Access Attacks
* Access attacks exploit known vulnerabilities in authentication services, FTP services, and web services. The purpose of these types of attacks is to gain entry to web accounts, confidential databases, and other sensitive information.

**Examples of Access Attacks**
1. **Spoofing attacks**
* The threat actor device attempts to pose as anoteher device by falsifying data.
    * IP spoofing
    * MAC spoofing
    * DHCP spoofing
2. **Trust Exploitations**
* Uses unauthorized previleges to gain access to a system.
3. **Port Redirection**
* Uses a compromised system as a base for attacks against other targets. i.e: using ssh on a compromised computer to get to host A.
4. **Man-in-the-middle Attacks**
* A threat actor positioned between two legitimate systems to read or modify the data that passes between them.
5. **Buffer Overflow**
* The threat actor exploits the buffer memory and overwhelms it with unexpected values.

## Social Engineering attacks
* Social engineering is an access attack that attempts to manipulate individuals into performing actions or divulging confidential information. Some social engineering techniques are performed in-person while others may use the telephone or internet.

### Social engineering attacks example:
1. **Pretexting**
    * A threat actor pretends to need personal or financial data to confirm the identity of the recipient.
2. **Phishing**
    * A threat actor sends fraudulent email which is disguised as being from a legitimate, trusted source to trick the recipient into installing malware on their device, or to share personal or financial information.
3. **Spear phishing**
    * A threat actor creates a targeted phishing attack tailored for a specific individual or organization.
4. **Spam**
    * Also known as junk mail, this is unsolicited email which often contains harmful links, malware, or deceptive content.
5. **Something for Something**
    * Sometimes called "Quid pro quo", this is when a threat actor requests personal information from a party in exchange for something such as a gift.
6. **Baiting**
    * A threat actor leaves a malware infected flash drive in a public location. A victim finds the drive and unsuspectingly inserts it into their laptop, unintentionally installing malware.
7. **Impersonation**
    * This type of attack is where a threat actor pretends to be someone they are not to gain the trust of a victim.
8. **Tailgating**
    * This is where a threat actor quickly follows an authorized person into a secure location to gain access to a secure area.
9. **Shoulder surfing**
    * This is where a threat actor inconspicuously looks over someone's shoulder to steal their passwords or other information.
10. **Dumpster diving**
    * This is where a threat actor rummages through trash bins to discover confidential documents.

## Denial of Service Attacks
* Overwhelming Quantity of Traffic
    - The threat actor sends an enormous quantity of data at a rate that the network, host, or application cannot handle.
    - This causes transmission and response times to slow down.
    - It can also crash a device or service.
* Maliciously Formatted Packets
    - The threat actor sends a maliciously formatted packet to a host or application and the receiver is unable to handle it.
    - This causes the receiving device to run very slowly or crash.

### Distributed Denial of Service Attacks
* it originates from multiple, coordinated sources.
*For example, A tIreat actor builds a network of infected hosts, known as zombies. The threat actor uses a command and control (CnC) system to send control messages to the zombies. The zombies constantly scan and infect more hosts with bot malware. The bot malware is designed to infect a host, making it a zombie that can communicate with the CnC system. The collection of zombies is called a botnet. When ready, the threat actor instructs the CnC system to make the botnet of zombies carry out a DDoS attack.*
